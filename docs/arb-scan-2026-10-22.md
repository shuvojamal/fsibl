## What this means

- Exchanges' prices typically differ by **0.01–0.05%**. The two trades
  needed to capture a gap cost about **0.15%** in fees (0.05–0.2% per side on
  the cheap exchanges, 1.2% on Coinbase). The gap is almost always smaller
  than the fees.
- The widest gap in five minutes was ADA: buy on Binance at 0.2154, sell on
  HTX at 0.216009, **+0.28% gross**. After fees it was still −0.017%. And
  the amount available at those prices was 81 ADA, about 17 USDT, so even a
  free trade would have made 5 cents.
- The exchange that most often showed the best price (MEXC, in 400 of 670
  scans) is also the one with the lowest fees, which is why its quotes sit
  slightly off the others. That's not an inefficiency, it's the fee priced
  in.
- Gaps above 0.1% gross appeared 34 times out of 670 (5%), lasted one scan
  (3 seconds) or less, and every one was below the fee hurdle.

## What a real arbitrage operation would need

- Capital parked on every exchange, in both USDT and each coin, so both
  legs fill in milliseconds. Moving coins between exchanges takes minutes
  and costs a withdrawal fee, far longer than any gap lasts.
- Fee tiers well below retail: at 0.02% per side the hurdle drops to 0.04%
  and some of the gaps above become real, but only for traders doing tens
  of millions of dollars a month in volume.
- Servers co-located with the exchanges. Firms already do this, and they
  are the reason the gaps are this small.

The "$68 → $750,000 from price gaps" story is not possible with retail fees
and public APIs. The scanner is still useful: run it for a few days
(`arb_logs/scans.csv` keeps every reading) and you'll see for yourself what
the market actually offers.
