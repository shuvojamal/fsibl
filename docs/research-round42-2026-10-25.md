# Research round 42: the CFA post's 30 DSE blue chips in our data

The owner shared a LinkedIn post (Md Sajib Hossain, CFA) with total market
returns of 30 DSE blue chips from 2001 (or listing) to 2025, counting cash
dividends, bonus shares, splits and rights: 2.2% to 24.8% a year (City Bank
22.1%, Olympic 24.8%, Marico 17.2%, BRAC Bank 18.4%, BATBC 18.3%).

**Our data, 2013-01 .. 2026-01, price + bonus only** (`research/results/research42.out`):
median share +3.5%/yr; best Eastern Lubricants +20.3%, BRAC Bank +16.4%,
Marico +15.8%, Berger +13.8%, Reckitt +12.8%, BSC +11.5%, Olympic +11.2%;
banks low on price alone (Dutch-Bangla +1.3%, Prime +0.1%, City +5.8%,
Pubali +4.8%), because banks pay most of their return as cash dividends.
None of the 30 lost money over the 13 years.

**Equal-weight basket of all 30, yearly rebalancing, 0.7% costs:**

| | 2013-10..2019 | 2020..2026-01 | whole |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 blue chips | +9.5%/yr, drop −23% | +7.1%/yr, −33% | +8.6%/yr, −33% |
| DSEX | +2.1%/yr, −31% | +2.4%/yr, −37% | +2.2%/yr, −43% |

Add typical cash dividends (roughly 4-6% a year for this group) and the
basket's total return is around 13-15% a year, in line with the post.

**Why the post's figures are higher than ours on price alone:**
1. Cash dividends: for banks the larger part of the return (City Bank paid
   Tk 11,451 in cash per original share).
2. The period: from 2001, at prices near book value (City Bank traded at
   1.0× NAV), through the 2009-10 boom; our data starts after the 2010 crash.
3. Hindsight in both: the list is today's 30 best companies, ranked by the
   value they created. Companies that looked like blue chips in 2001 and
   later failed are not on it (round 41 showed a price-based screen could
   not pick the lasting ones in advance).

**Meaning.** Holding a spread of strong, dividend-paying DSE companies for
many years has worked: about 9% a year on price + bonus since 2013, roughly
4 times DSEX, with smaller drops, plus dividends. Choosing which companies
qualify needs fundamentals (earnings, dividends, valuation versus NAV), not
price signals. No pass bar (hindsight basket, not a strategy test).
